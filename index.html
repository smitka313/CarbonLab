Peat Bog Carbon Lab

In an Arctic peat bog, plants pull carbon out of the air and store it in their bodies. When they die, the cold, soggy ground keeps them from rotting, so they pile up as peat over thousands of years. Change the conditions and see whether the bog stores carbon or releases it.

The same substances appear in every row, but the arrow points the opposite way. The outputs of photosynthesis are the inputs of respiration, and the outputs of respiration are the inputs of photosynthesis. Photosynthesis stores the sun's energy in glucose. Respiration and burning release that energy and return the carbon to the air. Peat builds up only when photosynthesis runs faster than its opposites.

Real-world connection · Arctic wildfires

Zombie fires

Some Arctic peat fires never really go out. After the flames die in the fall, the fire keeps smoldering underground all winter, hidden under the snow. In spring it bursts back to the surface. Scientists call these overwintering or "zombie" fires. They have come back in Alaska, Canada, and Siberia in recent years, as warmer summers dry out and thaw more peat.

Investigations to try

  • High tiltSet the tilt to 24.2°, like about 10,700 years ago. What happens to summer sunlight, temperature, and photosynthesis in the Arctic?
  • Low tiltSet the tilt to 22.1°. How much of the peat freezes into permafrost? Why can't decomposers or fire reach frozen peat?
  • Why peat builds upLower the water level from 80% to 10%. What happens to the oxygen in the peat, and to decomposition?
  • Peat fireSet burning to 60%. Try it with a wet bog, then a dry bog. Which one releases more CO₂?
  • Opposites cancel outFind settings where photosynthesis and decomposition are equal and there is no fire. What happens to the peat? Why does carbon stop building up?
  • Compare the outputsDecomposition and burning both release CO₂. What inputs do they share? How is the energy released differently?
  • Zombie fireStart a fire, then raise the water level a little at a time. At what water level does the fire stop surviving the winter? Why does water put it out?
  • ThawChoose "Arctic heat wave." Is the bog still a carbon sink, or is carbon being released to the atmosphere?
Bonus · Earth science connection

Carbon dioxide and global warming

Photosynthesis pulls CO₂ out of the air. Decomposition and burning put it back. Peat, coal, oil, and gas all formed from ancient plants, so burning them releases carbon that photosynthesis stored long ago. When more CO₂ goes into the air than plants and oceans can remove, it builds up and traps heat.

Why CO₂ traps heat

Sunlight passes through the air and warms the ground. The ground gives off heat as infrared energy. CO₂ molecules absorb that heat and send some of it back down, like a blanket.

Why it matters for peat

Warming thaws permafrost and dries out bogs. Decomposers wake up, and dry peat can burn, even as zombie fires that last through the winter. Both release more CO₂, which causes more warming. Scientists call this a feedback loop.
